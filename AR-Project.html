Business Context

Why Accounts Receivable Analytics?

Accounts Receivable represents money a business is owed by customers for goods or services already supplied on credit. Sales do not automatically translate into cash received. When invoices remain unpaid, businesses can experience cash-flow pressure, delayed collections and increased credit exposure.

An outstanding-balance report tells management how much money is owed, but not necessarily how old those balances are, how effectively collections are performing, or which customers need attention first. This project connects those questions through financial KPIs, ageing analysis, payment behaviour and a documented customer-risk framework.

Beyond Visualisation

The End-to-End Data Preparation Journey

The project covers the steps required to turn differently structured source data into a consistent analytical model. Dashboard development is one part of the process, not the entire solution.

Stage 01

Inspect the Source Data

Review customer records provided in five different formats, together with annual invoice and payment CSV files.

Stage 02

Standardise Customer Records with Python

Use Python and pandas notebooks to standardise column names, trim whitespace, harmonise customer-segment values and standardise region names.

Stage 03

Validate Data Quality

Inspect duplicate customer identifiers, conflicting records, null values and blank or whitespace-only values before customer reconciliation.

Stage 04

Reconcile the Customer Master

Establish a consistent customer structure for CustomerID, CustomerName, Region, CustomerSegment and CreditLimit. Reconciliation supports consistent customer identity and credit attributes across the analytical model.

Stage 05

Consolidate Invoice and Payment Data

Combine annual invoice files into a consolidated invoice fact table and annual payment files into a consolidated payment fact table.

Stage 06

Build the Data Model and Calendar

Establish the customer dimension, invoice and payment facts, and a calendar dimension covering 1 January 2023 through 31 December 2026.

Stage 07

Implement Business Rules and DAX

Translate the documented definitions for outstanding AR, overdue exposure, ageing, collection rates, DSO, credit utilization and customer risk into calculated columns and measures.

Stage 08

Develop and Publish Power BI Reports

Present executive KPIs, customer exposure and risk, and historical trends through three Power BI reports intended for interactive exploration.

Why Standardisation Was Necessary

The same business value can be represented differently across source systems. For example, “Large Enterprise” and “LARGE ENTERPRISE” should not remain inconsistent simply because they originated in different files. Python notebooks standardised relevant fields and prepared the datasets for reconciliation.

Why Validation Was Necessary

Customer identifiers were normalised by trimming whitespace and converting them to uppercase before duplicate checks. The notebooks also inspected null values, blanks and potentially conflicting records. Standardisation makes records more consistent; validation identifies issues requiring further review.

Data modelling decision: the standardised and reconciled customer master provides a common customer dimension. Invoice and payment transactions are consolidated separately because they represent different transaction levels. This helps prevent inconsistent customer attributes and unreliable customer-level aggregations.

Data Modelling

Why Consolidated Fact Tables and a Calendar Matter

🧾

Invoice Fact

Annual invoice files for 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 are consolidated into one transaction layer. This supports consistent calculations for invoice amounts, due dates, outstanding balances and ageing.

💳

Payment Fact

Consolidated payment transactions support collection totals, invoice settlement analysis, final payment dates and payment-delay calculations. An invoice may receive multiple payments, so the model must respect transaction detail.

📆

Calendar Dimension

A calendar from 1 January 2023 through 31 December 2026 provides a consistent date structure for filtering, monthly and annual reporting, and historical trend analysis.

Why this matters: consolidating annual transaction files reduces the need to maintain separate year-specific calculations. A dedicated calendar supports consistent time-based analysis, while distinct invoice and payment facts respect different levels of transaction detail.

Business Logic

Business Rules That Drive the Analysis

The value of the reports depends on translating transactional data into consistent, meaningful business measures. These examples explain the principal calculations and how they support receivables analysis.

Outstanding Accounts Receivable

Outstanding AR = Invoice Amount − Total Payments

Represents the unpaid balance remaining on invoices and provides a starting point for receivables exposure analysis.

Overdue Accounts Receivable

Outstanding balance on invoices past their due date at the cut-off

Uses the fixed historical cut-off of 31 July 2026. Fully paid invoices are not treated as overdue merely because their due dates have passed.

Ageing and Ageing Buckets

Current · 1–30 · 31–60 · 61–90 · 91–120 · More than 120 days

Groups receivables by overdue duration, helping management identify where older outstanding balances are concentrated. Fully paid invoices are not assigned an ageing bucket.

Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)

DSO = (Average AR ÷ Credit Sales) × Number of DaysAverage AR = (Opening AR + Closing AR) ÷ 2

Estimates how many days of credit sales are represented by average receivables. A higher DSO can indicate a lengthening collection cycle and should be interpreted alongside other AR measures.

Collection Rate

Collection Rate = Amount Collected ÷ Amount Due for Collection × 100

The documented working implementation uses invoices due on or before 31 July 2026 for the amount-due population. Collection rate should be considered alongside the absolute uncollected amount.

Payment Days and Payment Delay

Payment Days = Final Payment Date − Invoice DatePayment Delay = Final Payment Date − Due Date

Negative delay indicates early payment; zero indicates on-time payment; positive delay indicates late payment. Actual payment delay remains blank where a final payment date does not exist.

Credit Utilization

Credit Utilization % = Outstanding AR ÷ Credit Limit × 100

Shows how much of a customer's approved credit limit is represented by outstanding receivables. High utilization becomes more meaningful when combined with overdue exposure and payment behaviour.

Credit Limit Breach

Credit Limit Breach = Outstanding AR > Credit Limit

Identifies customers whose outstanding receivables exceed their approved credit limit under the project's defined rule.

On-Time Payment Percentage

On-Time Payment % = On-Time Paid Invoice Count ÷ Paid Invoice Count

Adds a payment-behaviour perspective by measuring the proportion of paid invoices settled on time.

Customer Exposure & Risk

Risk Is More Than a Large Outstanding Balance

The documented framework combines credit exposure, payment behaviour and collection risk. A customer is not automatically classified as critical simply because its outstanding balance is large.

01 — Credit / Overdue Risk

Credit Utilization % is greater than 90% and Overdue AR is greater than zero.

02 — Payment Behaviour Risk

On-Time Payment % is below 50%, the customer has at least one unpaid or partially paid invoice, and at least one relevant outstanding invoice in the 61–90, 91–120 or more-than-120-day ageing categories.

03 — Collection Risk Severity

Collection risk is classified as Low, Medium, High or Critical according to the project's defined Collection Rate thresholds.

04 — Critical Risky Customer

The customer must satisfy the Credit/Overdue Risk condition, the Payment Behaviour Risk condition, and have High or Critical collection risk. All three conditions are required.

Why this approach? The combined framework makes risk more explainable by showing the conditions contributing to the classification. It distinguishes customers with high credit exposure from customers who also demonstrate problematic payment behaviour and collection risk.

Power BI & DAX

From Business Definitions to Analytical Measures

DAX measures implement the business rules in the Power BI model and respond to the applicable reporting context, including customer selection, date filters and other report filters.

📐

Receivables Measures

  • Outstanding AR
  • Overdue AR
  • Overdue Rate %
  • Opening and Closing AR
  • Average AR
📊

Collection Measures

  • Total Collections
  • Collection Rate %
  • DSO
  • On-Time Payment %
  • Average Payment Delay
🛡️

Credit & Risk Measures

  • Credit Utilization %
  • Credit Limit Breach
  • Customer eligibility
  • Payment-behaviour risk
  • Critical Risky Customer

The model uses both invoice-level calculated columns, such as Days Overdue, Ageing Bucket, Payment Status, Payment Days and Payment Delay, and dynamic measures that aggregate or calculate values in the current report context. These serve different purposes within the analytical model.
